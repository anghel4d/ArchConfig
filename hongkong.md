Hong Kong, Hong Kong...

It's a wonder I made my flight out at all. Kowloon City was a dizzying labyrinth of soot-tainted walls and concrete. In the fetid moisture of a polluted shower that would never end, I made my way from shop to shop, looking for a buyer who'd take my the polished steel so tightly wrapped around my wrist. And with the turbulence wracking my plane from side-to-side, conspiring to derail the pen with which I write, I feel I must emphasize just how greedily that dreadful Hong Kong shroud guarded the precious rays of the sun. So jealously so that only now, soaring far above the clouds, am I once again graced by its light after four interminable days of darkness. 

Down there, under the malecifent glow of too many LEDs, I could read the details of that watch more closely. Haphazard scratches now quite visible, catching the multicoloured (with its awful GREEN and BLUE) light at egregerious angles, making every fault all the more apparent. A corner gouge in the steel of the case, and a couple more nicks of the bracelet here and there. The ceramic dial and sapphire glass, though, were as perfect as new, and under their pearlescent sheen one could read those letters: R - O - L - E -X. A meaningless string of characters, as it almost turned out, or in any case a substantially mythologized one. The fable of its value, or the fable of Hong Kong's famously deep liquidity, were about to be proven most faulty. 

"Sorry ! Price too high. Bye bye!" That was the entirety of the first appraisal.

"We don't buy," said the second, then "maybe 100'000 (Hong Kong Dollars, this was nearly $8000 USD below what the internet convinced me was fair market value)," said the third. I don't bother to recall the fourth and fifth, which were little different.

This city was supposed to be THE PLACE for this kind of thing. The Free Port of the world! The oldest grey market to still exist at all, FREE from the overreach of taxes and duties, it was meant to have the most unimpeded valuations out of nearly anywhere you could go. And it turns out that perhaps it did, because what was most disconcerting was just *how many* of these Rolexii were piled and stacked so haphazardly in every display. Value? Exclusivity? These might as well grow on trees, or be fished out of the Tai Tei Puck river or whatever the fuck the locals sail their dingy fishing boats over. Losing count of how many times I spotted the exact same model I so desparately wished to be rid of -- that gold-steel and shiny black dial taunting me in its commonality -- often two or three of the same were literally side by side right by the window of every shop. And there were at least a hundred of these shops. 

The profanity of it was insulting. Luxury watches are for niggers.

Eventually I *did* find one place that was unlike all the rest. A rather conspicuously bulletproof glass door swinging into a narrow wedge, two women and one barely old male behind the counter that traced the entire store's length, in the middle of which was an electronic cash-counter like the one they have at banks. In one corner of the customer's side of the store, was a mound of those iconic green boxes stacked all the way to the ceiling. One of the few, actively operating -- one of *those*.
